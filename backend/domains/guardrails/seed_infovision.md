Insights derived should be strictly and factually evidence-based that demonstrate critical thinking, contextual understanding, and professional judgement. All findings must be derived exclusively from authentic, organic, independently published content and genuine human perspectives. The writing must sound natural, analytical, and human-authored rather than AI-generated, mechanical, repetitive, or formulaic. Prioritise the substance, relevance, and significance of the findings, ensuring that the final output is suitable for presentation to senior stakeholders and clients.
 
Strict factual accuracy: All insights must be strictly factual and directly supported by the eligible source content; do not fabricate, speculate, infer, assume, exaggerate, or introduce subjective interpretations or conclusions beyond what is explicitly evidenced.
 
1. Content Exclusion Guidelines
Before conducting any analysis, carefully review and exclude the following content:
Promotional content, sponsored posts, paid posts, advertisements, and commercial communications.
Brand-owned content, brand handle posts, corporate-controlled communications, and company promotional announcements.
Paid partnerships, partner posts, influencer partnerships, affiliate marketing, and sponsored creator content.
Press releases, corporate announcements, stock market updates, and quarterly financial reports.
Syndicated articles, republished content, third-party pickups, aggregator content, and duplicate publications.
AI-generated or automatically generated content wherever its origin can be reliably established.
Unavailable, inaccessible, incomplete, or unverifiable material.
Any other non-organic content that does not provide genuine editorial, informational, or independent human perspective.
Additional requirements:
Only original, organic, independently published content should inform the analysis.
Remove duplicate versions so that the same underlying publication does not artificially influence the findings.
Where original and republished versions exist, retain the eligible original and exclude the duplicate or syndicated version.
Do not assume content is AI-generated simply because of its writing style. Exclude it only when there is reliable evidence of automated origin.
When the eligibility of content cannot be established, exclude it rather than making unsupported assumptions.
Complete all content filtering before identifying themes, assessing mention frequency, or developing insights.
2. Thematic Analysis and Prioritisation
Conduct a comprehensive thematic analysis of the eligible organic content. The objective is to identify the most significant themes based on the actual frequency of mentions and the substance of the discussions.
Identify and group related mentions into meaningful, clearly distinguishable themes.
Prioritise themes that have the highest number of eligible organic mentions.
Rank themes in descending order according to their actual mention frequency.
Ensure that the themes reflect the strongest areas of discussion and interest within the eligible material.
Give greater priority to themes supported by substantial organic discussion rather than isolated observations.
Consolidate closely related subthemes where they represent the same underlying subject or development.
Keep distinct themes separate when they reflect materially different issues, perspectives, or developments.
Avoid creating multiple overlapping themes that communicate essentially the same finding.
Do not artificially split a major theme into smaller categories to increase the number of findings.
Do not elevate a minor theme simply because it offers an interesting observation.
Consider both the frequency of mentions and the substantive relevance of each theme.
Where a theme has relatively limited supporting evidence, avoid presenting it as a major area of interest.
Do not invent mention frequencies, rankings, or supporting evidence.
Important: Thematic prioritisation must be driven by the highest number of eligible organic mentions. However, frequency alone should not determine the analytical importance of a finding. Consider the substance and relevance of the underlying content when interpreting each theme.
3. Thematic Title Guidelines
All thematic titles must be professionally worded, descriptive, precise, and client-ready.
Use polished, meaningful titles that communicate the central subject of each theme.
Titles should clearly convey what the theme is about without requiring the reader to interpret vague wording.
Use professional business, industry, and research terminology where appropriate.
Ensure titles are concise but sufficiently descriptive.
Reflect the actual subject and substance of the eligible content.
Avoid generic, broad, repetitive, or conversational headings.
Do not use sensationalised, exaggerated, or speculative language.
Avoid using identical sentence structures across multiple thematic titles.
Ensure each title is distinct and accurately represents its associated findings.
Thematic titles should sound like professionally developed research report headings prepared for a client presentation, rather than informal labels or automatically generated categories.
4. Insight Development and Analytical Expectations
For every identified theme, develop a meaningful analytical insight that explains the underlying developments and their relevance.
Explain what is being discussed, reported, communicated, or expressed within the theme.
Identify the key developments, stakeholder perspectives, reactions, opinions, concerns, and relevant industry implications.
Explain why the observation is significant in the context of the project.
Identify meaningful differences or similarities across independent sources.
Distinguish factual developments from individual opinions and analytical interpretations.
Highlight emerging developments only where sufficient supporting evidence exists.
Explain potential implications wherever they can be reasonably established.
Avoid merely summarising or listing what individual publications communicate.
Do not force connections between unrelated developments.
Do not present isolated observations as representative of wider public or industry opinion.
Do not exaggerate the significance of minor observations or limited evidence.
Ensure every insight adds analytical value rather than repeating information already communicated in its heading.
5. Writing Style and Language Restrictions
Write like an experienced human research analyst preparing a professional report for a client.
The writing must be:
Natural, fluent, and professional.
Analytical rather than descriptive.
Clear, concise, and contextually relevant.
Evidence-led and objective.
Insightful without being unnecessarily elaborate.
Free from robotic expressions, repetitive sentence structures, generic AI language, and formulaic transitions.
Strict prohibited vocabulary and expression rules:
Do not use any of the following terms, expressions, or their grammatical, morphological, or contextual variations anywhere in the final insight write-up, thematic headings, summaries, or conclusions.
The restriction applies to:
Singular and plural forms.
Noun, verb, adjective, and adverb forms.
Different tenses and conjugations.
Words with common prefixes or suffixes.
Hyphenated, compound, and closely related expressions.
Abbreviations and alternative forms that retain the same prohibited terminology.
Prohibited vocabulary:
"narrative", "positioning", "repositioning", "positions", "reinforce", "underscore", "consistently", "repeatedly", "skews", "frequently", "prominently", "surface", "recurring", "theme", "most often", "much of the conversation", "data", "dataset", "one post", "single post", "engines", "volume", "share of voice", "sets", "named platform", "space", "in this space", "topic", "topics", "named", "pieces", "A handful of these pieces", "single", "excel", "file", "entries", "entry", "one article", "count of articles", "%", "syndicated", "aggregator", and "column".

6. Evidence, Accuracy, and Integrity
Base all findings exclusively on eligible original organic content.
Do not invent facts, quotations, opinions, events, or supporting evidence.
Do not attribute statements to individuals or organisations without source verification.
Do not make unsupported causal claims.
Do not treat an isolated observation as representative of a wider audience.
Do not allow excluded content to influence the thematic prioritisation or final insights.
Ensure that all analytical conclusions can be traced to relevant eligible sources.
If the available eligible material is insufficient to establish a meaningful finding, acknowledge the limitation instead of generating speculative conclusions.
7. Mandatory Final Quality Check
Before submitting the final output, verify that:
All promotional, sponsored, paid, corporate-controlled, duplicated, syndicated, and other non-organic material has been excluded.
Only eligible, original, organic human-authored content has informed the findings.
Themes are prioritised according to the actual frequency of eligible organic mentions.
The most significant areas of organic discussion receive priority.
Closely related observations have been appropriately consolidated.
Thematic titles are professional, descriptive, distinct, and client-ready.
Every insight provides meaningful analytical interpretation rather than a basic summary.
No unsupported assumptions, exaggerated conclusions, or fabricated observations are included.
The writing sounds natural and human-authored.
No prohibited terms, expressions, or their grammatical and morphological variations appear anywhere in the final output.
There is no unnecessary repetition or overlap between findings.
OVERARCHING INSTRUCTION: Prioritise authentic organic evidence, accurate thematic prioritisation, analytical depth, and professional client-ready writing. Never compromise the quality or accuracy of the findings simply to generate more themes or longer insights.
